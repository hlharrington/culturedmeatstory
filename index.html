Cultured meat, in seven stops

From a hungry planet to a hybrid burger. Follow the dirt trail from the top, and hover over (or tap) each glowing icon to read its stop.

1 Start at stop one and work down

  1. A hungry planet

    Meat is a staple in the diets of many people around the world, and as the global population increases, so does meat’s demand.

    Unfortunately, the production of meat is very bad for the environment. Around half of all habitable land on the planet is used for agriculture. Of that half, three quarters are used just for livestock to make meat!

    The amount of land used is even larger than the area of the United States, China and India combined!

  2. Life on factory farms

    In order to produce large amounts of meat, livestock are made to live in cramped and dirty conditions on large factory farms. This causes the animals to become stressed and ill.

  3. Meat grown in a lab

    Thankfully, scientists are working on an incredible method to make meat that doesn’t have the same problems. Using cells harmlessly collected from animals, meat can be grown in a lab.

  4. Why cultured meat?

    This technology is often called cultured meat, and it has many advantages over normal meat. It requires 90% less land area and water usage and can even produce meat faster than normal farming. Because only meat is grown by the cells, no animals need to be slaughtered in order for it to be produced.

  5. The cost problem

    The equipment and ingredients needed to create the cultured meat are expensive and hard to make in large amounts. Until scientists come up with better technology, it is unlikely that you will see it in the store.

  6. Will people eat it?

    There is also concern of how willing people will be to eat cultured meat. Despite the advantages, being made in a lab attracts many strong judgments from consumers.

  7. The hybrid solution

    To solve these issues, companies are exploring making hybrid cultured meat products. These products contain only a small amount of cultured animal products which are combined with plant-based ingredients to lower the price but better the flavor of plant-based products. Would you try a hybrid cultured meat product?

Your turn: grow a burger

Scientists make cultured meat in four main steps. Play through them in order to grow your own.

  1. 1Collect

Collect a few cells

Scientists start with a tiny sample of cells from a cow. The cow isn't hurt and stays on the farm.

Your job: tap the glowing spot on the cow to collect the sample.

Feed the cells

Cells need food to grow. Scientists put them in a warm tank called a bioreactor and add a nutrient broth. Each feeding lets the cells split and double.

Your job: add broth until the tank holds 32 cells.

Cells in the tank: 1

Build on a scaffold

Muscle cells need something to hold onto, like a tiny jungle gym called a scaffold. As the cells stick to it, they line up and grow into muscle fibers.

Your job: tap each dashed strand to grow muscle on it.

Fibers grown: 0 of 5

Cook and serve

Finally, the grown muscle is pressed into a patty and cooked, just like any other burger.

Your job: start cooking, then press Stop while the marker is in the green zone.
